# AgroAI Agricultural Knowledge Base — Updated 2024-25

## Government Schemes

PM-KISAN (Pradhan Mantri Kisan Samman Nidhi) provides Rs. 6,000 per year to small and marginal farmers in three equal installments of Rs. 2,000 each, transferred directly to Aadhaar-linked bank accounts. The 19th installment was released in February 2025, benefiting over 9.4 crore farmers. To register, farmers can visit the nearest CSC, Patwari, or the official portal at pmkisan.gov.in with their Aadhaar card, bank passbook, and land ownership documents.

PMFBY (Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana) is India's crop insurance scheme with premium rates of 2% for Kharif crops, 1.5% for Rabi crops, and 5% for commercial and horticultural crops. The government pays the remaining premium. From 2024-25, claim settlement has been made faster through satellite imagery and remote sensing. Farmers must report crop loss within 72 hours using the Crop Insurance App or by calling the toll-free helpline 14447.

Minimum Support Prices for 2024-25 crop year: Wheat Rs. 2,275 per quintal. Paddy Rs. 2,300 per quintal. Maize Rs. 2,225 per quintal. Soybean Rs. 4,892 per quintal. Groundnut Rs. 6,783 per quintal. Cotton (medium) Rs. 7,121 per quintal. Mustard Rs. 5,950 per quintal. Gram Rs. 5,440 per quintal. These are declared by the government to ensure farmers get a fair price.

The Soil Health Card Scheme provides free soil testing and nutrient recommendations. From 2023, the frequency was extended to one card every three years instead of two. Farmers can visit any KVK or agriculture department office. The card shows levels of nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, and 9 micronutrients, plus recommendations for the specific crop.

e-NAM (National Agriculture Market) now connects over 1,363 markets across 23 states and 3 Union Territories. Farmers can register using their Aadhaar and bank details at any mandi or enam.gov.in. The platform allows electronic bidding and direct payment to bank accounts. In 2024-25 the platform integrated with ONDC (Open Network for Digital Commerce) allowing farmers to reach even more buyers.

RKVY (Rashtriya Krishi Vikas Yojana) allocated Rs. 1,01,321 crore in the Union Budget 2024-25 for agriculture and allied sectors. It supports natural farming, digital agriculture missions, vegetable clusters, and farmer producer organisations.

Kisan Credit Card provides short-term crop loans up to Rs. 3 lakh at 7% interest. With the Prompt Repayment Incentive of 3%, effective rate is just 4% per year. The KCC limit was extended to cover allied activities like fisheries and animal husbandry from 2023. Apply at any nationalised bank, cooperative bank, or regional rural bank with land documents and Aadhaar.

Agriculture Infrastructure Fund has disbursed over Rs. 55,000 crore for 60,000+ projects including cold storages, warehouses, primary processing units, and custom hiring centres. The fund offers 3% interest subvention and credit guarantee. Small and marginal farmers, FPOs, and SHGs can apply through the AIF portal at agriinfra.dac.gov.in.

PM Pranam (Pradhan Mantri Programme for Restoration, Awareness, Nourishment and Amelioration of Mother Earth) was launched in Budget 2023 to incentivise states that reduce chemical fertilizer use. States that reduce subsidised fertilizer consumption get 50% of the savings back as grant for natural farming programmes.

Natural Farming (Prakritik Kheti) is being promoted extensively since 2023. The government targets converting one crore farmers to natural farming by 2025-26. Key inputs are Bijamrit (seed treatment), Jeevamrit (microbial fertilizer from cow dung and urine), mulching, and whapasa (alternating wet and dry soil moisture). Zero external inputs beyond cow dung and urine.

PM Kisan Maandhan Yojana is a pension scheme giving Rs. 3,000 per month to small and marginal farmers above age 60. Monthly contribution ranges from Rs. 55 to Rs. 200 depending on age of entry. Apply at any CSC with Aadhaar, bank passbook, and land documents.

Rashtriya Gokul Mission allocated Rs. 2,400 crore for 2021-26 period. Focuses on breed improvement through AI (Artificial Insemination) using sexed semen to produce 90% female calves. Farmers can get AI done free at home through Gokul Gram centres. Indigenous breeds like Sahiwal, Gir, and Tharparkar are being promoted for higher milk yield.

## Crop Conditions and Requirements

Rice is India's most important staple crop. It needs temperatures between 21°C and 37°C, high humidity, and 100 to 200 cm of rainfall. Grows well on heavy clay or silty soils with pH 5.5 to 6.5. In 2024-25, varieties like Pusa Basmati 1718 and MTU 1010 are widely recommended. SRI (System of Rice Intensification) can increase yield by 20 to 30% with less water.

Wheat is sown from October to December in North India. Grows best at 15°C to 25°C in well-drained loamy soil with pH 6.0 to 7.0. HD 3385 and WH 1270 are top recommended varieties for 2024-25 offering resistance to yellow rust. Sow at 100 to 125 kg seed per hectare. First irrigation at 21 days (crown root stage) is critical.

Cotton grows at 21°C to 37°C on deep black soil. In 2024-25, Bt cotton still dominates 95% of the area. For Pink Bollworm management the government is introducing Bollgard III varieties. Avoid monoculture — rotate with wheat or chickpea. Harvest when 60% bolls open; avoid harvesting in morning dew.

Sugarcane requires 24°C to 37°C with high humidity. New varieties Co 0238 and CoPant 10225 give 80 to 100 tonnes per hectare. Ratoon management (not replanting but growing from existing roots) saves cost. Drip irrigation in sugarcane can save 40% water and increase sugar content by 0.5 to 1%.

Maize has become India's third largest cereal. Grows at 18°C to 27°C in 75 to 100 cm rainfall. High-yielding hybrids like DKC 9141 and P3522 are popular. Baby corn, sweet corn, and popcorn are gaining in value and can give 3 to 4 times the income of grain maize. Requires one deep ploughing, 2 to 3 light irrigations.

Potato grows at 15°C to 25°C in loose sandy loam soil. In 2024-25, Kufri Ganga and Kufri Pushkar are recommended for heat tolerance and late blight resistance. Average yield is 25 to 30 tonnes per hectare. Seed potato from certified sources reduces disease. Cold storage at 2°C to 4°C extends shelf life to 9 months.

Banana is grown year-round in southern India. Grand Naine is the most popular variety for export. Farmers adopting tissue culture plants and drip irrigation report 25 to 40 tonne yields vs 20 tonnes from traditional sucker planting. NABARD provides 25% subsidy for banana cultivation infrastructure.

Tomato is grown in both seasons. Pusa Rohini, ArkaSaurabh, and hybrids like Badshah F1 give 60 to 80 tonne yields. Main diseases in 2024 are Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus (TYLCV) and early blight. Use virus-resistant hybrids and whitefly control to manage TYLCV.

Onion prices fluctuated severely in 2024. Government introduced buffer stock operations, buying from farmers at minimum prices and releasing during shortages. Bhima Super and Agrifound Light Red are popular varieties. Proper curing (drying for 10 to 14 days) and storage in well-ventilated stores reduces losses.

Soybean is the oilseed crop of central India, mainly Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra. JS 335 remains popular but JS 9560 and Pusa Soybean 12 are better for high yield and disease resistance. YMV (Yellow Mosaic Virus) is the main disease — use whitefly management from day one.

Mustard is India's top Rabi oilseed. PM-MOVCDNER scheme supports mustard farming in northeast India. In 2024-25, Rajasthan, Haryana, and MP are main producers. Pusa Double Zero Mustard 33 has almost zero erucic acid and is preferred for healthy edible oil. APH 1 (aphid resistant) variety is available.

Groundnut is a key Kharif oilseed also used as food. TG 37A, Kadiri 6, and GG 20 are top varieties. Pod development requires calcium in soil — apply gypsum (500 kg/ha) at peg penetration stage. Aflatoxin contamination in stored groundnut is a major issue — dry below 9% moisture.

Coffee is a major plantation crop grown in Karnataka, Kerala, and Tamil Nadu. It requires a hot and humid climate with temperatures between 15°C and 28°C and rainfall of 150 to 250 cm. It grows best on well-drained, forest loamy soils rich in humus and iron, mainly on hill slopes under shade. Robusta and Arabica are the main varieties grown.

Tea is a crucial beverage crop requiring a warm, moist, frost-free climate throughout the year with temperatures of 20°C to 30°C and 150 to 300 cm of annual rainfall. Frequent showers evenly distributed over the year ensure continuous growth of tender leaves. It thrives on well-drained, deep, friable loamy soils rich in humus and organic matter on hill slopes. Major producing states include Assam, West Bengal, and Tamil Nadu.

## Soil Management and Fertility

Soil health has degraded significantly in India due to overuse of urea and neglect of organic matter. Average organic carbon in Indian soils is 0.4% compared to desired 1.5%. Building organic matter through compost, green manure, and reduced tillage is the most important long-term investment a farmer can make.

Carbon farming is gaining attention in 2024-25. Farmers who increase soil organic carbon get better yields, reduce fertilizer needs, and may soon earn carbon credits. CSA (Climate Smart Agriculture) practices including zero tillage wheat, direct seeded rice, and cover cropping qualify for carbon credits.

Biostimulants like seaweed extracts, amino acid-based products, and humic acid are increasingly used to improve soil biology and plant health. These are different from fertilizers — they improve root growth, stress tolerance, and nutrient uptake efficiency. Several government-approved products are available at agriculture shops.

Nano-urea developed by IFFCO was launched in 2021 and is being promoted heavily from 2023. One bottle of Nano Urea (500 ml) is equivalent to one bag of conventional urea. It is sprayed on leaves and increases nitrogen use efficiency from 30% to 90%. Price is Rs. 225 per bottle. Available at IFFCO cooperative outlets.

Nano DAP, also from IFFCO, was approved in 2023. It replaces conventional DAP for phosphorus supply through foliar spray. Both Nano Urea and Nano DAP reduce soil pollution, groundwater contamination, and are more economical.

Soil pH management: most Indian soils are either acidic (northeast, Kerala, parts of Karnataka) or alkaline (Punjab, Haryana, UP, Rajasthan). For acidic soils below pH 5.5, apply lime at 2 to 4 tonnes per hectare, preferably before Rabi crop. For sodic soils above pH 8.5, apply gypsum at 5 to 10 tonnes per hectare along with green manure to reclaim land.

## Irrigation and Water Management

PMKSY (Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana) allocated Rs. 93,068 crore for 2021-26. The Har Khet Ko Pani component aims to bring 76 lakh hectares under stable irrigation. Drip and sprinkler systems are subsidised at 55% for general farmers and 75% for small/marginal farmers and SC/ST.

Micro-irrigation is expanding rapidly. India now has over 74 lakh hectares under drip and sprinkler irrigation. The National Mission for Sustainable Agriculture promotes micro-irrigation, soil health management, and water conservation under one framework.

Direct Seeded Rice (DSR) saves 25 to 35% water compared to transplanted rice. Also saves on labour costs significantly. After initial weedicide application, two to three irrigations less are needed. Punjab and Haryana are promoting DSR aggressively to address groundwater depletion.

Per Drop More Crop is a sub-scheme under PMKSY specifically for micro-irrigation. Farmers get subsidy for drip systems, fertigation equipment, soil moisture sensors, and water storage tanks. Apply at the district agriculture department or on the PM-KISAN portal.

Rainwater harvesting through farm ponds is heavily subsidised under MGNREGA and state schemes. A typical farm pond of 0.1 hectare can store 1,000 to 1,500 cubic metres of water, enough to save a standing Rabi crop from a dry spell. Construction cost subsidy varies by state — check at the gram panchayat.

## Pest and Disease Management 2024-25

Fall Armyworm (FAW) is the biggest new pest threat, attacking maize, bajra, and sugarcane. It arrived in India in 2018 and is now widespread. Control with Spodoptera-specific biopesticides (spinosad, Baculovirus), pheromone traps (10 per hectare), and spraying emamectin benzoate at 0.4 ml per litre in early morning.

Whitefly-transmitted disease complex has become severe in cotton and vegetables since 2022 due to warmer winters. Remove and burn badly infected plants. Use yellow sticky traps, reflective silver mulch, and imidacloprid or thiamethoxam as 3 to 4 applications during critical periods.

Biological control agents being promoted in 2024-25: Trichogramma (for egg parasitism of borers), Trichoderma (soil fungal disease control), Beauveria bassiana (insect pathogen), NPV nucleopolyhedrovirus (for caterpillar control). These are cheaper than chemical pesticides and safe for humans and environment.

Drone-based spraying is rapidly expanding. Kisan Drones can cover 25 to 30 acres per day vs 5 acres by manual spray. Government provides 40 to 50% subsidy on drones for FPOs (Farmer Producer Organisations). Custom hiring of drones is available in most districts at Rs. 500 to 700 per acre.

Herbicide-resistant weeds are emerging in wheat fields of Punjab and Haryana. Phalaris minor (little seed canary grass) is resistant to isoproturon. Rotate herbicide modes of action and include clodinafop combined with metsulfuron for effective control. Zero Tillage wheat also suppresses Phalaris minor.

## Market and Income

Farmer Producer Organisations (FPOs) allow small farmers to pool resources, negotiate better prices, and access markets collectively. Government provides Rs. 15 lakh to each new FPO plus equity grant and credit guarantee. Over 10,000 FPOs have been formed. To register, contact NABARD, SFAC, or state cooperative department.

Agri-commodity prices for 2024-25 are available in real time on the eNAM portal, Agmarknet, and the Commodities app. Farmers can see prices at nearby mandis before deciding where to sell. RFID tags on produce bags are being piloted to enable seamless tracking from farm to buyer.

APMC (Agricultural Produce Market Committee) reforms since 2020 allow farmers to sell outside APMC mandis, directly to processors, exporters, and retailers. Electronic trading, contract farming, and direct marketing are now legal in most states.

Exports of agricultural commodities from India hit a record USD 53 billion in 2023-24. Rice, spices, marine products, buffalo meat, and fresh vegetables are major exports. APEDA (Agricultural and Processed Food Products Export Development Authority) provides Rs. 50 to 75 per kg assistance for certified organic exports.

## Animal Husbandry 2024-25

Milk production in India crossed 230 million tonnes in 2023-24, making India the world's largest milk producer. Per animal productivity still lags — HF and Jersey crosses produce 20 to 25 litres per day while average Indian cow gives 5 to 7 litres. Improving breed through AI with sexed semen and better feed can triple income.

Lumpy Skin Disease (LSD) caused major cattle losses since 2022. The government rolled out free Lumpy Pro VacInd vaccine. Farmers must vaccinate all cattle annually. LSD spreads through biting insects — use mosquito repellent sprays and move cattle out of wet, shaded areas during monsoon.

Pashu Kisan Credit Card works like Kisan Credit Card but for animal husbandry — provides credit up to Rs. 1.60 lakh for cows, Rs. 60,783 for buffaloes, and Rs. 4,063 per layer for poultry. Apply at any bank with animal ownership documents.

PM Matsya Sampada Yojana allocated Rs. 20,050 crore for fisheries development 2020-25. Subsidises fish seed, aquaculture ponds, cages, fishing boats, cold chain infrastructure, and insurance. Freshwater aquaculture of Rohu, Catla, and Tilapia can give 5 to 8 tonnes per hectare per year, giving farmers a strong second income.

## Technology in Farming 2024-25

Digital Agriculture Mission launched in 2023 with Rs. 2,817 crore allocation. Key components: AgriStack (digital identity and land records for every farmer), Krishi Decision Support System (crop advisory using satellite data), and Digital Crop Survey (replacing manual crop cutting experiments with digital counting).

AgriStack creates a Farmer ID linked to land records, KCC, soil health card, and PM-KISAN data on a single platform. This allows farmers to access all government benefits through one digital identity, reducing paperwork significantly.

Weather-based advisory services are now freely available via the Meghdoot app (by ICAR and IMD), Kisan Suvidha app, and Fasal Bima app. Farmers get block-level weather forecasts, crop advisories, and market prices on their mobile. Rainfall and temperature data help decide sowing, spraying, and harvesting dates.

Precision agriculture tools like soil sensors, drone scouting, and satellite-based crop monitoring are becoming more accessible. ISRO's Bhuvan Fasal app provides free satellite crop maps. Startups like DeHaat, AgroStar, and Ninjacart are bridging gaps in input supply, advisory, and market linkage.

Kisan Call Centre number is 1800-180-1551, available toll-free in 22 languages, 7 days a week. Farmers can get immediate advice on crop problems, pest attacks, scheme eligibility, and market information.
